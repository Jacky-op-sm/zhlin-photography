Now, let’s turn to the book. I came across it after finishing Welcome to the Hyunam-Dong Bookshop and searching online for something similar. It appeared on Goodreads, and I added it to my reading list.

I love bookstores; they are the places I visit most frequently when living in a city. Second to that are Apple stores. I imagined this book would provide a similar sense of healing as the last one. Now that I have finished it, I can say my expectations were mostly met.

The book has five chapters, each telling a different story—from young people in their twenties who have just graduated to individuals in their sixties who have just retired. They are all experiencing challenging times in life.

Some characters are more relatable to me—for example, the young woman in the first chapter who struggles with her daily life and wants to switch jobs, or the NEET who loves drawing manga but hasn’t succeeded in that field. Others, such as the married woman juggling her job and newborn child or the retired man, are simply stories for me to read.

What I like about this book is the mysterious librarian who always knows the pain people are suffering and dispenses simple yet effective remedies like an experienced doctor. I found emotional comfort in the recurring sentence that describes the librarian’s appearance. If possible, I would like to advise the author to write another chapter exploring the story of this mysterious librarian.

What I do not like, though I don’t hate it, is that the book remains shallow and shares the same problems as other modern popular books. The themes the author explores are scattered—career, marriage, raising a child, and life’s purpose. Each theme is touched upon briefly and thus not explored in depth. Another aspect of this book is that the stories follow a similar pattern, like riding the same roller coaster repeatedly. Initially, the protagonist is always in some life crisis, then is led to the librarian and finds a solution by connecting with a book, almost like a miracle.

I felt heartened when I saw these people overcome their troubles, but deep down, I had to admit that their resolutions were too good to be true. But at least, reading it spurs me to make some changes in my life, whether it’s giving deep thought to my future career or getting rid of bad habits to stay healthy.
